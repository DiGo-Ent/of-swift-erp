Frequently Asked Questions

Clear Answers & Project Inquiries

Detailed, transparent explanations regarding the BIOSHIELD AI concept, detection methodology, and technical architecture.

What is BIOSHIELD AI?
BIOSHIELD AI is a conceptual project showcasing how modern computer vision and deep learning techniques could assist growers, gardeners, and researchers in identifying crop diseases through leaf image analysis. It illustrates the complete pipeline from leaf image upload to disease diagnosis and plant-care recommendations.
How does the proposed disease detection process work?
The proposed process operates in five clear stages: (1) A leaf image is captured or uploaded, (2) the image is preprocessed and resized to 224×224 pixels, (3) a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) extracts multi-scale visual features such as color, edges, and lesion patterns, (4) the model outputs probability scores across known disease classes, and (5) the predicted disease, confidence level, and relevant agronomic care advice are displayed.
Can I upload a leaf image on this website?
No. This website is a static frontend demonstration built exclusively with HTML5 and CSS3. There is no file upload handler, server storage, or image processing backend connected. The interactive demo section provides a visual simulation of how an uploaded leaf and its inspection card would appear in a complete system.
Does the website perform real AI analysis?
No, this website does not perform live AI analysis. It is an educational and design showcase that presents the user experience and architectural blueprint for an AI-based system. The example results, such as the 90% confidence score for Early Blight, are static illustrations created to show how information would be structured.
What does the confidence score mean?
In a machine learning classification model, the confidence score represents the statistical softmax probability assigned to a specific class. For example, a 90% confidence score for Early Blight suggests that the network found strong pattern alignment with trained Early Blight features. If all probabilities fall below a predetermined threshold, a responsible model labels the specimen as "Unknown" to prevent false diagnoses.
Which plant diseases are illustrated?
The showcase illustrates four foundational botanical categories commonly explored in benchmark datasets: Healthy Leaf, Early Blight (Alternaria solani), Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans), and Leaf Spot (Septoria / Cercospora). These demonstrate how visual symptoms vary from healthy foliage to severe fungal and oomycete infections.
Can this project be connected to a real AI model in the future?
Yes, absolutely. The user interface layout, visual hierarchy, and component cards have been architected to accommodate live REST API endpoints or TensorFlow.js / ONNX web-runtime inference in future development phases, should a trained computer vision model be connected.
What datasets are typically used to train such agricultural AI models?
Benchmark models typically utilize publicly available plant pathology datasets such as the PlantVillage dataset (containing over 54,000 curated leaf images across 14 crop species and 38 diseases) as well as field-collected datasets featuring variable background lighting and soil clutter to enhance real-world generalization.
What are the best practices for photographing leaves for computer vision?
For optimal accuracy in computer vision models: (1) Position the leaf flat against a neutral or plain background whenever possible, (2) avoid harsh shadows or direct sunlight glare, (3) ensure the primary lesion or discoloration is sharply in focus, and (4) capture the leaf at an angle perpendicular to the camera sensor.
